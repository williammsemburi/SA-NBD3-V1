# Joint uncertainty methods

## Overview

Version 1 uses one joint simulation. Each draw starts from the validated deterministic checkpoints, varies the evidence-supported quantities, reruns the relevant analytical operations in their original order, and then constructs the same final cause hierarchy used by the point-estimate database.

For a reported quantity $D$, the 95% interval is the empirical 2.5th and 97.5th percentile across completed joint draws.

## Completeness and under-reporting

Positive pre-freeze S2 information is collapsed to one death-weighted aggregate multiplier for each province. The nine provinces are treated as clusters in the exact bootstrap distribution of the national weighted mean. One shared national factor is drawn and applied to African natural-cause deaths before the HIV/AIDS model is rerun. The deterministic relative pattern between provinces is retained.

This is an empirical proxy for uncertainty in the national completeness level. The source completeness files do not supply a full sampling variance model.

## Injury composition

The injury surveys provide uncertain measurements of a closed 15-cause composition. In each draw:

1. NIMS uses its observed count information;
2. IMS and FAMHIS use Kish count-equivalent information derived from their weights;
3. each survey composition is sampled from a Dirichlet distribution centred on its prepared composition;
4. the sampled compositions pass through the same additive-log-ratio interpolation and triangular smoother as the deterministic estimate; and
5. the resulting fractions divide the fixed total injury envelope.

Survey uncertainty therefore affects specific injury causes but cancels when all injury causes are summed. No independent path variance is added between surveys.

## HIV/AIDS

Complete fitted coefficient vectors are sampled jointly from the Stage 05 variance-covariance matrices. The full HIV/AIDS reallocation is rerun after completeness scaling, including background mortality, zero-prevalence intercepts, age weights, caps, exclusions, population-group allocation and return of residual deaths.

Because deaths moved to HIV/AIDS are removed from their donor causes, the complete cause vector retains the negative covariance generated by the reallocation. Antenatal prevalence remains fixed because no approved variance-covariance input is supplied.

## Ill-defined and garbage causes

The deterministic expert target lists remain unchanged. For each multi-target rule, one non-empty subset of the approved targets is selected, and the same ordered proportional algorithm is rerun. Single-target rules remain deterministic. Redistribution changes cause composition but preserves the demographic-cell total and the natural/injury envelopes.

## Aggregation and covariance

All-cause deaths are calculated within every draw:

$$T^{(b)}=\sum_c D_c^{(b)}.$$

The reporting layer maps the complete analysis-cause vector to final ZA causes within each draw and only then calculates quantiles. It therefore preserves covariance across HIV/AIDS donors, redistribution recipients, injury causes, provinces, South Africa and population groups. It does not add independent cause-specific variances after aggregation.

## Current scope

The configured draw outputs support Person estimates for provinces, South Africa and national population groups for the reporting age groups retained in `config/uncertainty_joint.yml`. Deaths and corresponding crude rates receive intervals. ASRs and YLLs remain point estimates. The total injury envelope, S1, NPR-specific variance and antenatal prevalence remain fixed where the supplied inputs do not support a defensible stochastic model.
